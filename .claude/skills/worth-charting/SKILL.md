## The Six Pillars

Apply all six to every stock. A strong chart has multiple pillars aligned. A weak chart has pillars in conflict.

---

### Pillar 1 — The 150-Day Moving Average ("The Truth Line")

Worth's signature indicator. Preferred over the standard 200-day because it smooths enough to eliminate noise while remaining sensitive enough to catch trend changes in real time.

**The four states:**

| Price vs. 150-Day MA | Structural Read | Action Bias |
|---------------------|----------------|------------|
| Above a **rising** 150-day | Structurally bullish | Hold / Add on tests |
| Below a **declining** 150-day | Structurally bearish | Avoid / Trim |
| **At** the 150-day (within ±2%) | Inflection point — binary outcome | Watch for directional resolution |
| Extended >20% above or below | Mean reversion probability rises sharply | Take profits or setup forming |

**Distance rule:** Calculate how far price is from the 150-day as a percentage. Extended stocks (>15–20% from the 150-day) revert. This is both a warning signal for longs extended above and a setup signal for longs extended below.

**Companion indicator:** Always pair the 150-day read with the **weekly RSI price oscillator** to confirm overbought/oversold:
- Weekly RSI >70 + price >15% above 150-day = **overbought — take profits**
- Weekly RSI <30 + price in falling wedge at support = **oversold — setup forming**
- Weekly RSI declining from high + rising wedge = **exhaustion — sell signal**

Also note the 50-day and 200-day MAs for context:
- 50-day rising above 200-day (Golden Cross) = bullish structural shift
- 50-day declining below 200-day (**Death Cross**) = Worth's most bearish structural signal — do not fight this

### Pillar 2 — Volume Precedes Price

**The most important principle in Worth's framework.** He states: *"The study of volume and price-volume correlation is the single most important thing one can do if one wants to engage in pattern recognition and studying price action trying to profit in the market."*

*"In markets, volume precedes price"* — a deliberate philosophical parallel to "existence precedes essence."

**Reading volume signals:**

| Volume Behavior | Price Behavior | Worth's Read |
|----------------|---------------|-------------|
| Expanding | Rising | Real breakout — commit capital |
| Expanding | Falling (spike) | Capitulation — potential reversal setup |
| Contracting | Rising | Exhaustion / false advance — warning to exit |
| Contracting | Falling | Sellers exhausting — bullish divergence forming |
| Flat/average | Any | No conviction — stand aside |
| Institutional (13F-confirmed) | During base formation | Highest conviction buy signal |

**The key insight:** Declining volume on a price decline is NOT bearish — it means sellers are losing conviction. This is often the setup for a reversal. The danger is rising price on declining volume — that's the false advance Worth watches for.

### Pillar 3 — Geometric Pattern Recognition ("Cave Drawings")

Worth reads charts as visual geometry — the same patterns his predecessors identified in the "cave drawings" of market history. Pattern recognition is the foundation of all major decisions.

#### Reversal Patterns

**Head & Shoulders (H&S) — Bearish Top**
- Structure: Left shoulder → head (highest point) → right shoulder (lower than head) → neckline
- Confirmation: Close below neckline on above-average volume
- Measured target: Height of head above neckline, projected DOWN from neckline break
- Stop for shorts: Close back above right shoulder high

**Inverse Head & Shoulders (IH&S) — Bullish Base**
- Structure: Left shoulder → head (lowest point) → right shoulder (higher than head) → neckline
- Confirmation: Close above neckline on above-average volume — Worth's highest-conviction buy signal
- Measured target: Height of head below neckline, projected UP from neckline break
- Institutional volume during base formation (visible in 13F filings) = compound confirmation
- Stop: Close back below right shoulder low

**Double Top — Bearish Reversal**
- Two price peaks at approximately the same level with a valley between
- Confirmation: Break below the valley ("neckline") on volume
- Measured target: Height of the pattern projected down

**Double Bottom — Bullish Reversal**
- Two price troughs at approximately the same level
- Confirmation: Break above the peak between the two lows on volume

#### Continuation / Compression Patterns

**Ascending Triangle — Bullish**
- Flat horizontal resistance + rising lower trendline
- Breakout target = height of triangle added to breakout point
- Wait for breakout above flat resistance on expanding volume before committing capital

**Descending Triangle — Bearish**
- Flat horizontal support + declining upper trendline
- Resolves to the downside approximately 65% of the time
- Breakdown target = height of triangle subtracted from support
- Institutional selling during formation confirms

**Symmetrical Triangle / Pennant — Neutral compression**
- Converging trendlines with no slope bias
- Resolves in the direction of the prevailing trend (with volume)
- Do not anticipate direction — wait for the break

**Rising Wedge — Bearish despite rising price**
- Both support and resistance lines rising, but converging (support is steeper)
- Resolves to the downside approximately 70% of the time — do not be fooled by rising prices
- Confirmation: Break below the lower wedge line
- Classic signal that Worth uses to flag tops and call for profit-taking

**Falling Wedge — Bullish despite falling price**
- Both support and resistance declining but converging
- Accumulation is occurring during the decline
- Resolves to the upside — breakout from upper wedge line = entry
- Declining volume during the wedge = sellers exhausting = adds conviction

**Cup & Handle — Bullish Continuation**
- Rounded base (the cup) followed by a shallow pullback (the handle)
- Buypoint = breakout above the handle's resistance on expanding volume
- Failed handle (breaks below cup lip) = sell signal

**Rectangle / Channel — Range-bound**
- Parallel support and resistance containing price
- Trade the range until a directional break with volume
- Break of well-defined channel = measured move equal to channel width

#### Structural Signals

**Death Cross:** 50-day crosses below 200-day — Worth's most bearish structural signal. The 50-day becomes resistance on all subsequent rallies. Do not fight this — it takes months to base out after a death cross.

**Golden Cross:** 50-day crosses above 200-day — structural bullish shift. Add on 150-day tests.

### Pillar 4 — Gap Analysis (Gaps Are Price Magnets)

*Most gaps fill eventually.* Gaps represent "unfinished business" and act as price magnets — they pull price back toward them over time.

| Gap Type | Occurs When | Behavior | Action |
|----------|------------|---------|--------|
| **Breakaway** | Price breaks free from a base | Signals trend initiation; may not fill for months | Trade in direction of break |
| **Runaway / Continuation** | Mid-trend acceleration | Confirms trend; becomes support (in uptrend) or resistance (downtrend) | Hold position |
| **Exhaustion** | End of a trend, extreme move | Fills quickly — reversal signal | Fade after confirmation |
| **Common** | Random, inside trading range | Fills within days; noise | Ignore |

**Practical rules:**
- Unfilled gap BELOW current price = support magnet — watch for test
- Unfilled gap ABOVE current price = resistance magnet — limits upside until filled
- Stock that can't fill a gap on the way up is showing distribution
- Stock that fills an upside gap quickly after a breakout = breakout may be failing

### Pillar 5 — Relative Strength (RS)

Compare the stock's price performance to:
1. Its sector ETF (e.g., XLK for tech, XLV for healthcare, XLE for energy)
2. The S&P 500 (SPX)

| RS Signal | Meaning | Action |
|----------|---------|--------|
| RS rising AND above its own moving average | Stock leading sector and market | Overweight |
| RS breaking out from a base | High-conviction initiation signal | Buy on confirmation |
| RS declining despite flat/rising stock | Distribution — relative weakness | Warning; reduce |
| RS making new lows while price holds | Price will follow RS lower | Sell / avoid |
| RS breakout from base + IH&S in price | Worth's highest conviction setup | Full position on trigger |

### Pillar 6 — Weekly RSI Price Oscillator

Worth uses the **weekly RSI** (not daily) as his secondary confirmation tool alongside the 150-day MA. Weekly RSI removes noise and aligns with his 3–6 month intermediate-term focus.

**Key thresholds:**
- RSI above **70** on weekly chart = overbought condition — watch for rising wedge + volume divergence
- RSI below **30** on weekly chart = oversold condition — watch for falling wedge + volume divergence
- RSI declining from >70 while price makes a new high = **negative divergence** = sell signal
- RSI rising from <30 while price makes a new low = **positive divergence** = buy setup forming

**RSI is a secondary confirmation tool, not a standalone signal.** Always pair with the 150-day read and a geometric pattern to trigger.

## The Well-Defined vs. Ill-Defined Test (Binary Classification)

Before analyzing any chart, apply Worth's binary test:

**Well-Defined:** The chart is "speaking." Clean geometry, MAs in order, volume confirming, identifiable slope and pattern. Tradeable.

**Ill-Defined:** The chart is "mumbling." Tangled MAs, no clean pattern, random volume, price chopping in a wide range with no direction. No edge. Stand aside and do nothing.

Worth's rule: *"When the chart doesn't tell you something clear, it's telling you to do nothing."*

## Sell Signals — When Carter Worth Exits

Worth is equally focused on selling as buying. The six conditions that trigger a sell or profit-take:

1. **Extended above 150-day (>15–20%) + weekly RSI >70** = Overbought — take profits
2. **Rising wedge forming** — converging trendlines despite rising price = exit before the break
3. **Volume divergence** — price rising but volume declining over multiple sessions = false advance
4. **150-day break on volume** — structural trend change; every rally is now a sell
5. **H&S topping pattern confirmed** — neckline break on volume = full exit, consider short
6. **Death Cross** — 50-day crossing below 200-day = structural damage; the 50-day becomes resistance

## Classification System

Apply one classification to every stock analyzed. Use the exact symbols — they appear in the ARKVX Morning Briefing Worth Signal column.

| Symbol | Category | Criteria | Suggested Action |
|--------|---------|---------|-----------------|
| ✅ | **Well-Defined Uptrend** | Above rising 150-day, clean ascending channel or triangle, volume confirming, no death cross | Hold; add on 150-day pullbacks |
| ❌ | **Well-Defined Downtrend** | Below declining 150-day, lower highs and lows, descending geometry, death cross possible | Avoid new longs; trim existing |
| 🔄 Long | **Mean Reversion Long Setup** | Extended >15% below 150-day, falling wedge or IH&S forming, volume divergence (declining vol on decline) | Wait for trigger candle; define entry/stop/target |
| 🔄 Short | **Mean Reversion Short / Take Profits** | Extended >15% above 150-day, rising wedge, weekly RSI >70, volume fading | Tighten stops; reduce position; do not add |
| ⚡ | **Inflection / Basing** | Within ±5% of 150-day, triangle compression approaching apex, or cup forming handle | Stand aside until directional break; define the two outcomes and their targets |
| ⬜ | **Ill-Defined / Insufficient Data** | Tangled MAs, no clean pattern, random volume, or stock too new for MA structure (IPO <90 days) | No edge — stand aside entirely |
